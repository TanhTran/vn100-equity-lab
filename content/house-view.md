# Growth is running hot; the constraint has moved from demand to the balance of payments.

Real GDP expanded **8.18% y/y in Q2-2026**, a third consecutive quarter above 7.5%, with manufacturing doing the work — industrial production is up 12.7% y/y and the PMI has held above 50 for the whole of H1. Retail sales are compounding at 12.9% YTD. On the activity side there is nothing to fix.

The stress sits on the external and price side. H1-2026 imports of **US$283bn** against exports of **US$267bn** leave a **US$16.7bn goods deficit** — a genuine regime change after a decade of structural surpluses, and only partly offset by US$13.0bn of disbursed FDI. Headline CPI has cooled to 4.69% from 5.60% in May, but core at 4.50% is sticky and sits at the top of the tolerance band.

The SBV is therefore easing on rates and tightening on liquidity at the same time: refinancing rate unchanged at 4.50%, but OMO has been draining, reverse-repo outstanding is being rolled down and the dong has been walked past **26,490** at VCB — roughly 5% weaker than the central rate. FX, not inflation, is the binding constraint on further easing.

## The dong

VCB ask is pinned near the top of the ±5% band. A central-rate reset, not spot intervention, is the likely release valve.

## Import surge

Machinery and input imports are running ahead of exports. If it is capex it is benign; if it is inventory it is not.

## Core stickiness

Core at 4.5% leaves no room for a cut even as headline decelerates. Watch services and housing components.

## Credit pace

Credit is tracking ahead of a full-year path near 16%. Funding costs at the 1M tenor are already backing up.
