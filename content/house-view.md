# The FX valve was pulled — but the external squeeze keeps widening, and inflation refuses to break below 4.5%.

Growth is the easy part of this story. Real GDP grew **8.18% y/y in Q2-2026**, the fastest of a four-quarter run all above 7.5%, and the acceleration is real-time: industrial production is up **14.4% y/y in August** (from 9.3% in April), the manufacturing PMI printed **53.3**, its firmest of the year, and retail sales are compounding at **13.3% y/y**. On the activity side there is nothing to fix and nothing to stimulate.

The July view flagged that the SBV would relieve the dong through a central-rate reset rather than spot intervention — and that is exactly what happened. At end-July the VCB ask was pinned at **26,490**, **+4.55%** above a central rate of 25,338. The SBV then walked the central rate **up to 25,610** and spot eased back to **26,260**, cutting the premium to **+2.54%**; the free-market rate (25,890) now trades *below* the official ask. The immediate balance-of-payments squeeze is contained. But it was bought, not solved.

Underneath, the external drain keeps widening. Year-to-date through August, exports of **US$374.8bn** against imports of **US$395.3bn** leave a **US$20.5bn goods deficit** — up from US$16.7bn at mid-year — and the Q1 current-account surplus has collapsed to **+US$2.7bn** from +US$12.5bn a year earlier. **US$15.2bn** of disbursed FDI is doing the plugging. With IIP running at 14%, the import surge still reads as machinery and production inputs — the benign, capex version — but it is a drain all the same, and the buffer is thinning.

Inflation is the other constraint, and it is not co-operating. Headline CPI ticked back up to **4.89% in August** — from 4.45% in July — even as Brent kept *falling* to **US$88** from US$103 in March. With energy a tailwind, the re-acceleration is coming from food and services rather than oil: monthly CPI swung to **+0.47%** after two negative prints. Core is the calmer read at **4.55%**, but it too has held above 4.4% all year. Either way the SBV has no room to cut. Liquidity, meanwhile, is running two-speed: overnight interbank is flooded at **1.19%** while 1-month money sits at **6.7%** and 3-month at **7.1%** — far above the unchanged **4.50%** policy rate. The SBV is holding rates, drowning the overnight market, and letting the term curve and the trading band carry the FX adjustment.

For markets, the regime is unchanged in character and sharper in degree: the tail risk is external — the dong and the balance of payments — not demand. The sector map below follows directly. **Overweight** the domestic, VND-earning and funding-advantaged: banks with deposit franchises, the industrial–port–parks complex that the FDI-and-capex loop runs through, power, and staples with pricing power. **Underweight** the USD-levered and import-exposed — oil & gas into falling Brent, aviation, import-heavy discretionary retail — and the high-leverage, rate-sensitive names a firming term curve punishes. Stay **neutral on residential property**, where the Vingroup-led rally is already in the price. It is a relative call, into a tape pricing the growth but not yet the funding cost.

## Inflation won't break

Headline is back to 4.89% and core is stuck at 4.55% — both pinned above the 4.5% line. With Brent falling, the push is now food and services, not energy, and a +0.47% August print says it is not fading. No room to cut into an FX squeeze.

## The external gap

The YTD goods deficit is −US$20.5bn and still widening; the current-account surplus has thinned to +US$2.7bn. FDI disbursement is the offset that matters — watch whether it keeps pace with the import bill.

## Term funding, not overnight

Overnight at 1.19% says liquidity is ample; 1M–3M at 6.4–7.1% says it is not. The term curve is where the market is pricing FX and duration risk — the honest read on funding stress.

## The dong after the reset

Premium over the central rate is back to +2.54% and the free market has slipped below the official ask — pressure genuinely eased. But the reset spent policy room; another leg of import growth reloads the squeeze, and the next valve is narrower.
